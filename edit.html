02 Three Concepts For OBT

Three Opportunities to Grow Your Audience

One flagship series, one short taco drama, and one fast way to use the footage you already have. We film and produce at every level, so where you start is up to you.

01
Long-Form Episodic · Flagship, ongoing
"The First Year"
A woman who has been out of work for a year and has stopped telling people how long it has been. A man whose job went away and who has no idea what the next one is called. A twenty-year-old with no diploma, no plan, and a mother who keeps asking. All three end up at the same OBT workforce hub in Brooklyn.

The First Year is a documentary series that follows one of them at a time, over months rather than one afternoon. The participant is the protagonist. An OBT instructor or career coach is the guide who walks in and stays. You have done a version of this before, because the eight-episode OBT Alumni Interview series you filmed in 2018 already put graduates on camera telling their own stories.

You meet someone on their first day, when the classroom is still full of strangers. You watch the hard weeks, the exam they have to pass, and the morning they get ready for the interview. And you are there the morning they walk into a job that pays enough to live on.

Queer Eye (the Netflix series that rebuilds one person's life per episode with experts who genuinely help) pulled in millions of viewers by putting one real person and one real problem at the center of every episode. The First Year does that for work, and it turns the numbers you publish on your homepage into people an audience can follow and root for.
Why long-form episodic wins over time
One-off videos are not appointment content. A returning series is what gives people a reason to come back.
Trust builds in small moments repeated over time, and each episode is one more.
A year of episodes earns an audience that comes back, and every participant you follow is another season.
02
Short-Form Episodic · The Taco Drama
"Room 2B"
It is 8:40 on a Monday morning in the back office of an OBT workforce hub. Orientation starts at nine, the projector has picked this morning to stop working, and the employer who was going to speak has just texted to say they are on the wrong train.

A 'Taco Drama' is a short scripted series designed to be easy to consume on the go. Room 2B is a workplace comedy set in the back office of a job-training nonprofit, one room and one impossible morning per episode, with episodes that run a minute or two, shot vertical for phones and widescreen for YouTube and your website.

The cast is fixed, four people who share Room 2B, the back office behind the classrooms. Yvette has run the front desk for twenty years and knows which radiator to hit and when. Andre is the job developer who says yes to every employer before he looks at the room calendar. Priscilla knows every funder's reporting rule, obscure or not, and will tell you which one you are about to break whether or not you asked. And Curtis works the phones, calm and kind, with a headset held together by tape. Every week Andre's latest promise collides with Priscilla's rulebook, Curtis calls down the list, and Yvette gets the room turned around with a minute to spare.

Abbott Elementary (the comedy set in a Philadelphia public school) proved a workplace show can love the work while it laughs at the workday. And Superstore (the sitcom where the staff face one absurd situation a week and everything resets by the next episode) proved that an office with a new deadline every week always has another story. Anyone who has worked in a place like this gets ninety seconds they recognize, and everyone else gets a look at the office that keeps the programs running.
Why short-form drama is worth the bet
Entertainment is value given first. Nobody shares an ad, but people share a ninety-second comedy that made them laugh.
Trust builds in small moments repeated over time, and a taco drama episode is the smallest repeatable moment there is.
The world resets every episode. There is always another orientation to get ready for, so the show never runs out of stories.
03
From Footage You Already Have · fast
"The Library You Already Own"
There are 60 videos on your channel. Eight of them are the OBT Alumni Interview series from 2018, five more are the Meet our Graduates films, and the fortieth-anniversary and Voices of Impact material sits beside them. Across those two playlists, graduates say on camera, in their own words, what changed for them.

Nothing new needs to be recorded. We take the strongest moments from that library and build them into produced short films, with titling, motion graphics, and a clear arc from start to finish. One long interview becomes three or four short films.

The same library gives you a two-minute cut for the homepage, where graduates in their own recorded voices say what the training did for them.

Someone deciding whether to enroll, and a funder deciding whether to give, both get ninety seconds from the people who went through the programs.
Why this is the fastest place to start
The footage is already shot, so the only new cost is the edit.
The fastest way to start, because there is no shoot to schedule and nobody's time to book.
Start with story, follow with facts. A graduate saying what changed gives the numbers their meaning.
03 Lead With Value

The Legendeering Framework

The methodology behind every Talex production, and behind the three concepts above. Five directives, one core principle.

Legendeering is Talex's proprietary strategy. The core principle is straightforward: lead with value, always. The brand earns trust by giving without expectation, and that trust grows over time because people keep choosing to watch.

Five directives drive it:

  • Give Without Expectation. Value delivered without strings. The kind of generosity that builds loyalty money cannot buy.
  • Be Relentlessly Authentic. Say true things. Show real work. Speak like a human being.
  • It's Not About You. It's About Them. The audience has to come first. It's all about their wants and needs and making something they will choose to watch.
  • Start With Story, Follow With Facts. Story gives information shape, context, and emotional weight. Facts alone rarely move people.
  • Consistency Is Key. Trust is built in moments, repeated over time.

OBT is expert at getting New Yorkers trained, credentialed, and hired. Talex would add the video: a documentary series, a short scripted comedy, and new cuts from the footage you already have, each one built on the method above. The three concepts came straight out of it.
